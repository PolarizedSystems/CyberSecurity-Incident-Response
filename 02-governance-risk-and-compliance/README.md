## Overview

**Governance, Risk, and Compliance (GRC)** is a structured approach to managing organizational security, identifying and mitigating risks, and ensuring compliance with regulatory requirements and industry standards. It connects technical security controls with business objectives, accountability, and enterprise risk management.

GRC consists of three core pillars:

- **Governance:** Establishes security policies, organizational responsibilities, and oversight to align cybersecurity initiatives with business objectives.
- **Risk Management:** Identifies, assesses, prioritizes, and mitigates threats that could impact organizational operations and assets.
- **Compliance:** Ensures security practices align with applicable regulations, industry standards, and internal policies.

In this section, I explore how these principles support **enterprise security strategy, regulatory compliance, risk reduction, and informed decision-making**.

Understanding GRC strengthens the connection between technical security operations and business leadership, ensuring security decisions are both technically effective and aligned with organizational priorities.

### Learning Outcomes

This section covers the following pillars:

- Pillar 1 – Governance
- Pillar 2 – Risk Management
- Pillar 3 – Compliance & Resilience

After completing this section, you should be able to:

- Align governance frameworks, risk appetite and operational controls with business objectives
- Apply the four risk-handling strategies and document residual risk in a risk register
- Assess risk qualitatively and quantitatively (SLE/ALE/ARO) and run a business impact analysis
- Evaluate vendor, supply-chain and privacy risk
- Map security practices to regulatory obligations and plan certification and accreditation
- Plan and test business continuity and disaster recovery

Incident response does not begin when the alarm sounds — it begins with the governance decisions made months earlier. The severity scale a responder reaches for, the regulators who must be notified and on what clock, the backups that determine whether recovery takes an hour or a week, the contracts that decide who is liable for a third party's breach: all of it is set by the GRC program long before an incident occurs.
