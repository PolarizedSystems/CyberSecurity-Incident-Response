## Overview

The best analysis in the world is worthless if the people who need to act on it never understand it. Security work produces findings — vulnerabilities, incidents, compliance gaps — but findings only create value when they are *communicated* to the right audience, in the right language, at the right time. A critical vulnerability explained in raw CVE identifiers to a CFO lands as noise; the same finding framed as business risk gets a budget. This is the last mile of security, and it is the one that most often decides whether the work actually changes anything.

This section covers that discipline: turning technical findings into clear, audience-appropriate communication. It runs as a thread through everything else in this portfolio — the [vulnerability management](https://github.com/PolarizedSystems/Vulnerability-Management), [incident response](../03-managing-cybersecurity-incident-response/README.md), and [governance and compliance](../02-governance-risk-and-compliance/README.md) work all culminate in a report someone has to read and act on.

It is organized into two modules, matching the two broad streams of security findings:

| Module | Focus | What It Covers |
|--------|-------|----------------|
| **[1 · Communicating Vulnerability & Compliance Findings](01-communicating-vulnerability-and-compliance-findings/README.md)** | The proactive stream | Vulnerability management reporting · metrics & KPIs · compliance reporting · inhibitors to remediation |
| **[2 · Communicating Incident Response](02-communicating-incident-response/README.md)** | The reactive stream | Incident response reports & stakeholders · root-cause analysis & lessons learned · incident response metrics & KPIs |

### Learning Outcomes

This section covers the following modules:

- Module 1 – Communicating Vulnerability & Compliance Findings
- Module 2 – Communicating Incident Response

After completing this section, you should be able to:

- Write vulnerability-management reports that classify, prioritize and recommend without jargon
- Select and present the metrics and KPIs that matter to each stakeholder group
- Produce compliance reports that map controls to regulatory obligations
- Recognize and communicate the real-world inhibitors that block remediation
- Structure incident-response reports around the 5 W's and the facts stakeholders need
- Communicate root-cause analysis and lessons learned so they drive change
- Report incident-response performance with mean-time metrics (MTTD, MTTR)

## The One Rule That Runs Through Everything: Know Your Audience

Every topic in this section returns to a single principle — **the message must be shaped for the person receiving it.** The same finding is communicated very differently depending on who is listening:

| Audience | What They Need |
|----------|----------------|
| **Executives / C-level** (CISO, CTO, CFO) | A concise executive summary — business impact, risk, and decisions required; not the technical minutiae |
| **Department heads & managers** | Non-technical framing of what is affected and what it means for their operations |
| **IT security team** | The full technical detail — indicators, techniques, affected systems |
| **IT staff** (network/system admins, support) | Actionable, system-specific instructions |
| **Development** | Precise, technical findings tied to code and configuration |
| **Customers & the public** | Clear, measured communication about service impact — without jargon or unnecessary alarm |

Getting this right is the skill the whole section teaches: translating one set of facts into whatever form makes it useful to each audience.
